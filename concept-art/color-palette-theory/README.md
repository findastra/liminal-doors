# Color palette theory

Astra, 2026-10-29: "I really like these color contrasts ... I want these to be like the models of how we're going to do a
lot of the stuff." These flooded, tiled pool rooms are a color reference for her projects, not Doorspaces or floors.
Use them as models for how to light and color new scenes.

## The rooms

| File start | Room |
|---|---|
| `neon-pool-01-flooded-corridor` | The picture Astra loves (as she gave it), and colorways of it |
| `neon-pool-02-corridor` | A long flooded corridor, glowing slits on both walls, the door at the end |
| `neon-pool-03-stairs` | Wide tiled steps rise out of the water to the door |
| `neon-pool-04-colonnade` | Tiled pillars standing in the water |
| `neon-pool-05-skylight` | A tall narrow room lit from one slit in the ceiling |

## The palettes

Each room comes in the same six colorways. The rule behind all of them: the walls, the water and the door each get their
own hue, with strong contrast between them, and the light sources stay neon.

| Name | Walls / light | Water | Door |
|---|---|---|---|
| dream | violet and pink light | deep blue | lavender |
| acid | lime and magenta | teal-green | hot pink |
| fire-ice | orange and red | aqua | aqua |
| toxic | purple and green | violet | yellow |
| candy | yellow and hot pink | hot pink | teal |
| ultra-red | red | deep red | cyan |

P01's colorways keep the picture and give its walls, water and pink light their own new hues
(`../tools/ref-colorways-20261029.py`). P02 to P05 are Blender renders (`../tools/neon-pools-20261029.py`, Blender 4.5,
Cycles, about 12 s each on the GPU; the `PALETTES` table in it holds the exact colors).

P01 is the picture Astra gave on 2026-10-29; where it came from is not recorded here.
